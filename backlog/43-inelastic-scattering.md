# 43 · Inelastic scattering and Coulomb excitation (second slice)

**Priority:** P2 · **Size:** L · **Area:** Nuclear planner

> Outline only. Refine once the elastic slice (33–42) is done and the lessons from it are known.

## Why
The natural next step after elastic scattering: the beam particle (or the target nucleus) is left in an excited
state, so part of the kinetic energy goes into excitation. Below the Coulomb barrier this is Coulomb excitation, a
standard way of measuring collective properties of nuclei. It reuses almost all of the elastic slice: the kinematics
gain Q = −E*, and the app, report and exports stay the same.

## Scope (first ideas)
- Nuclear level data (energies, spins, parities, B(E2) values) from ENSDF, with provenance as in item 34.
- Inelastic kinematic lines in E vs θ and in the simulated spectra, for projectile and target excitation.
- Coulomb excitation cross sections in first-order semiclassical theory, with a "safe distance" check (closest
  approach well outside the nuclear surface) as a validity warning.
- γ-ray emission from the excited nucleus: energies with Doppler shift and Doppler broadening for given γ detector
  positions.
- Validation against GOSIA (the standard Coulomb-excitation code) and published Coulomb-excitation measurements.
